# geometry-fem (domain-independent finite elements, ADR 020)

Private package of the R3 analysis design and ADR 020. Not a registered editor or a stable public API. Depends on `@noble/hashes` only, never on a CAD, BIM, character or simulation package (the ADR 017 rule for `geometry-*`).

- `fem.ts` (R3a): `FemMesh` (tet4 or tet10 in VTK node order, millimetres; boundary faces as element + local face + shape-face tag; node chains per shape edge) and its immutable byte form FEM 1. `encodeFem` gives the same bytes for the same mesh; `decodeFem(bytes, limits)` checks the header, the section table (all eight sections, 8-byte aligned, no overlap), the JSON meta and every array (finite coordinates within ±10 km, indices in range, no repeated node in an element, a positive corner tetrahedron, no face shared by more than two elements, every boundary face on the outside and listed once, tags below the shape-face count, edge chains of 0 or ≥ 2 nodes) against `femLimits`, and refuses anything else with `FemAssetError` (`fem-asset-invalid`). Nothing is repaired. `femSha256` names the bytes as content does (`sha256:<hex>`). `localFaces` (face k is opposite corner k, wound outward), `tet10Edges`, `cornerVolume6`, `femVolume`.
- `primitives.ts` (R3a): `boxTetMesh({ size, divisions, type })`, a structured mesh of a box (six tetrahedra per cell around its main diagonal), its faces tagged x0, x1, y0, y1, z0, z1 (`boxFaces`) and its 12 edges as node chains (`boxEdges`). A fixture for the format and the solver, not a mesher; general meshes come from the `fem-mesher` module (R3b).
- `vtu.ts` (R3a): `writeVtu({ mesh, pointData, cellData })`, an XML UnstructuredGrid (version 1.0, little-endian, inline base64 with a UInt32 header) with VTK cell types 10 and 24 and Float64 fields of 1, 3, 6 or 9 components. Field names are limited to letters, digits, space and `_.-()`; a field whose length does not match, holds a non-finite value or repeats a name is refused.
- `sparse.ts` (R3d-1): `csrPattern` (3 DOFs per node by default, 1 for a scalar field; every node pair of an element coupled), `CsrMatrix` (`add`, `diagonal`, `multiply`) and `pcg`, Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients on a mask of free DOFs that stops at a relative residual or an iteration limit and says whether it converged. `pcg` takes a starting guess and, optionally, a preconditioner in place of Jacobi (R3i-1). `incompleteFactor(A, mask)` (R3g-1) is ILU(0) (an incomplete Cholesky for a symmetric matrix) on the pattern, retried with a growing diagonal shift if a pivot is not positive, used as a preconditioner.
- `elastic.ts` (R3d-1): `solveElastic({ mesh, material, loads, constraints, solver })`, linear static analysis of an isotropic linear elastic solid (4-point Gauss, exact for tet10 stiffness). Loads are mapped already: a total force over shape faces by area or over shape edges by length (consistent nodal shares of the quadratic triangle and line), a pressure on shape faces along the inward normal, gravity (consistent body-force shares). Constraints give components per DOF on the nodes of shape faces or edges and are eliminated. Before assembly it refuses a material outside E > 0, −1 < ν < 0.5 (`material`), no constraint (`no-constraint`), a target with no node or face (`empty-target`) and a constraint set that leaves a rigid motion free (`rigid-body-mode`, naming the free translations and rotations; `freeRigidModes`), checked on each connected piece of the mesh (`meshComponents`), so a piece nobody holds is refused however well another is held. A non-positive Jacobian is `mesh-quality`. The result has displacements, nodal stresses (each element's exact nodal values averaged), von Mises (`vonMisesOf`), principal stresses (`principalOf`, `principalStresses`), reactions, applied and reaction totals, what each load put on the mesh (force sum and area or length), constrained node counts, iterations, the residual and `converged`. `prepareElastic` (R3f-1) does the checks and the load and constraint mapping alone and returns the prescribed DOFs and the nodal forces, which `solveElastic` and the CalculiX export share.
- `calculix.ts` (R3f-1): `writeCalculixInp({ mesh, material, prepared, units })` writes a linear static study for CalculiX (C3D10/C3D4 in the same node order, `*BOUNDARY` per prescribed DOF, the consistent nodal forces as `*CLOAD`, U, RF and S requested; numbers in at most 20 characters, `calculixNumber`), in mm–N–MPa or m–N–Pa. `readCalculixFrd(text, nodes, units)` reads CalculiX's ASCII `.frd` (short or long node numbers): the DISP, STRESS and FORC blocks of the last step, each node exactly once, finite values, converted back to mm and MPa. Binary results, another node count, unknown node numbers, short records and missing blocks are refused with `FrdError` (`result-invalid`). Only declarative data is written; CalculiX itself is never bundled (U3). R3j-5: `writeCalculixModel({ nodes, parts, prescribed, force, equations, units })` writes any solid model: element blocks of C3D4, C3D10 or C3D15 each with its own material, and homogeneous `*EQUATION`s.
- `calculix-shell.ts` (R3j-5a): a shell for the Host. CalculiX's S3 locks in bending and leaves rotations out of `*EQUATION`, so `expandShell(mesh)` builds the 15-node wedges CalculiX would build for S6 (corners at −t/2, 0, +t/2 along the area-weighted node normal, straight mid-edges at ±t/2; refuses creases over 60° and mixed thickness with `host-shell`), `expandShellStudy` turns `prepareShell`'s DOFs into the wedges' (held translations on the middle node, held rotations on the top and bottom, a side held at its corners' mean or by an equation) and `shellResultFromFrd` folds the `.frd` back: translations, rotations n × (u_top − u_bottom)/t, three layers' stresses, reactions with moments, and force and moment scales from the layer forces for the 6-digit equilibrium check.
- `bonded.ts` (R3j-5b): `bondedHostModel(input)` makes bonded bodies one CalculiX model after `solveBonded`'s checks: each part its own block (tet, or a shell's wedges) and material, each bond's dependent nodes (a shell's three layers and the sides on the bond) tied by `*EQUATION`s, to a solid face's shape values at the node, or to a shell's middle layer plus h θ × n with θ from its top and bottom and its in-plane rotation (the wedges have no drilling DOF). `bondedResultFromFrd` moves each tied DOF's residual to what it follows (Tᵀ), then reads each part back.
- `calculix-modal.ts` (R3j-5c): modal models for the Host. `writeCalculixModel` with `modes` writes `*DENSITY` per part and a `*FREQUENCY` step asking for U and RF (mm only, held at 0, no force); `readCalculixModes(text, nodes, modes)` reads each MODAL frame (numbered 1, 2, … in order, frequency from the header, rising) with its DISP and FORC blocks. `calculixModalSummary` checks the modes from the `.frd` alone: the Rayleigh quotients |φᵀ F / λ − 1| and K-orthogonality |φᵢᵀ Fⱼ / √(λᵢ λⱼ)| from F = K φ; the effective masses use the browser's mass instead, φᵀ M r = Σ m_p φ_p with each node's row sum (`nodalMasses` in elastic.ts, ρ t A / 3 for a shell), so the held rows' coupling counts. `modalHostModel`/`modalResultFromFrd`, `shellModalHostModel`/`shellModalResultFromFrd` (`foldShellDisplacement`) and `bondedModalHostModel`/`bondedModalResultFromFrd` keep the browser solvers' refusals and result types; the total mass is the editor's own (ρ × volume, ρ t × area).
- `calculix-frame.ts` (CS4, docs/cad/construction-site.md CS-U5): a frame of beams for the Host. `checkFrameModel` checks a `FrameModel` as it arrives (nodes, groups of 2-node elements with one section by its properties — area, I₁₁ across the depth, I₂₂, torsion, extreme fibres c₁ and c₂ — one depth direction and one material, fixed nodes, nodal forces) and refuses anything else with `FrameModelError` (`frame-invalid`). `writeCalculixFrame` writes CalculiX's user beam `U1` (six DOFs a node, `*USER SECTION` A, I₁₁, 0, I₂₂, J, the depth direction, no offsets; not the expanded `B32R`, whose rigid-joint equations took over a minute for one storey) and asks for U only. `readCalculixFrame` reads the translations and rotations of every node from the `.frd` and recovers each element's end forces with its own stiffness (`frameElementForces`: N, and the two moments from the cubic through both ends; no load acts inside an element) and its largest extreme-fibre stress |N|/A + |M₁|c₁/I₁₁ + |M₂|c₂/I₂₂.
- `modal.ts` (R3g-1): `solveModal({ mesh, material, constraints, modes, solver, progress })`, the lowest natural modes of a held solid: `assembleStiffness` and the consistent mass `assembleMass` (ρ ∫ N_a N_b dV), the held DOFs eliminated, LOBPCG preconditioned by `incompleteFactor(K)` with a few guard vectors and soft locking, until every wanted mode's ‖Kφ − λMφ‖ / ‖Kφ‖ is below the tolerance. Each mode has its frequency (Hz), its mass-normalised shape, its residual and its effective mass fractions in x, y, z; the result has the total mass, the M-orthogonality and the constrained node counts. It refuses what the static solver refuses, a prescribed displacement other than 0 (`constraint-conflict`) and a density ≤ 0. `symmetricEigen` is the small dense (Jacobi) eigen solver. The LOBPCG itself is `lowestModes({ K, M, free, perNode, coordinates, modes, solver })` and the masses and orthogonality `modalSummary(M, eigen, perNode)` (R3i-1), shared with the shells. In Node, six modes of the FX4 cantilever take about 2 s on 1,025 nodes and 31 s on 6,561 nodes.
- `thermal.ts` (R3h-1): `solveThermal({ mesh, material: { conductivity }, conditions, solver })`, steady heat conduction with held temperatures (faces and edges), a heat flux into the body and convection h (T∞ − T) on faces. Power is mW, so k in mW/(mm·K) is W/(m·K), a flux in mW/mm² is kW/m² and h in mW/(mm²·K) is kW/(m²·K); temperatures are K. `assembleConduction` is k ∫ ∇N_a·∇N_b dV (one DOF per node), convection adds h ∫ N_a N_b dA, held temperatures are eliminated and the PCG starts from their mean. The result has the nodal temperature, the averaged flux −k∇T and its size, each condition's heat (reactions for a held temperature) and area, and the heat balance. It refuses k or h ≤ 0, no held temperature or convection (`no-constraint`), a piece with neither (`rigid-body-mode`), two temperatures on one node and an empty or unknown target. `boundaryFacePoints` and `boundaryFaceList` (elastic.ts) are the face quadrature and face lookup the elastic loads and the thermal conditions share.
- `shell.ts` (R3i-1): thin shells on flat three-node triangles with 6 DOFs per node (translations and rotations in global axes). `ShellMesh` has the midsurface nodes, the triangles (their order gives the normal), a thickness and a shape-face tag per triangle and node chains per shape edge. Each triangle is an Allman membrane (the LST with mid-side displacements from the corner rotations, 3-point rule; a small penalty on ω_i − ω(centroid) removes the equal-ω mode, `shellElementStiffness`) plus a DKT plate (Batoz, Bathe and Ho 1980), turned into global axes. `solveShell({ mesh, material, loads, constraints, solver })` takes face forces by area, edge forces by length, pressure against the triangle normal and gravity (lumped shares A/3 and l/2), and constraints per displacement and rotation component, eliminated; it solves by CG preconditioned by `incompleteFactor`. The result has displacements and rotations, the top, middle and bottom stresses as global tensors averaged at the nodes, von Mises on the top and bottom, reactions, force and moment totals (`shellEquilibriumCheck`) and the load checks. `prepareShell` refuses a material, a thickness ≤ 0 (`material`), a triangle with no area (`mesh-quality`), no constraint, an empty or unknown target, a conflict and a free rigid motion on any piece (`shellFreeRigidModes`, a held rotation holding the rotation modes, `shellComponents`). `solveShellModal` uses the lumped mass `assembleShellMass` (ρ t A/3 on the translations, ρ t³/12 · A/3 on the rotations) and `lowestModes`. Known answers FX6: the clamped plate +1.1%, the Scordelis–Lo roof −1.2% and the simply supported plate −0.14% on 16 × 16 grids, each under 0.5 s in Node.
- `shell.ts` (R3i-3): `shellMeshFromSurface(surface, thicknessMm)` turns the kernel's triangulated midsurface (`analysisSurface` with `idealisation: 'shell'`) into a `ShellMesh` with one thickness, refusing an empty surface, out-of-range tags or nodes and a thickness ≤ 0 (`RangeError`). `shellMeshQuality` gives the smallest angle, the largest aspect ratio and the triangles past `shellQualityMin` (2°, 30).
