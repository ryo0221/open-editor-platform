# cad-kernel-occt (CAD-T2b)

Private OCCT kernel adapter for the 3D CAD design. Not an accepted distribution: the D0 gate is still open and development uses the npm OpenCascade.js build recorded in D0.

- No npm dependency on OpenCascade.js. The domain-independent parts (build loading and verification, exception decoding, catalog resolution, the Worker's job order, mesh normals) live in [`geometry-kernel-occt`](../geometry-kernel-occt/README.md) (ADR 017) and are re-exported here under their CAD names; the STEP scan reads with `geometry-core`'s P21 reader. The Host supplies a `CadKernelBuild` (module URL, Wasm URL, `sha256:` build hash); `loadVerifiedBuild` refuses Wasm bytes with another hash, then compiles and instantiates them as separate steps (`kernelLoadMarks` in the Worker's performance timeline), and documents evaluate only on the build named by `kernelProfile.buildHash`.
- `evaluateCad`: full-graph evaluation in stable topological order. One valid solid per feature, result tolerance limit, decoded OCCT exceptions, per-body volume/area/bounds, model-space meshes with per-triangle face pick IDs, the pick table and edge polylines (below) (linear deflection 0.1% of each body's bounding-box diagonal, `displayDeflectionMm`), output limits. Every handle is deleted before returning. Persistent topology names (R1b): each feature names its output faces from its recipe (box `±x/±y/±z`, cylinder `side/top/bottom`, sphere `surface`, extrude and revolve `start`/`end`/`side:<profile element>`, polyline sides `side:v:<vertex ID>`), carried through placements and Booleans by the kernel's history (`ModifiedShape`, `Modified`, `IsDeleted`, `Generated`); a Boolean prefixes its tool's faces with `tool:<tool feature ID>`; a Boolean of several tools applies them at once (`SetArguments`/`SetTools`), so their order changes neither result nor names; a face several tools share is named `shared:` with all their names. Fillet and chamfer (R1c) resolve their stored references on their input's topology table and fail with `reference-ambiguous`/`reference-missing` and the broken references (`references`, with candidates and the reference that would pick each); a fillet or chamfer the kernel cannot build fails with `fillet-failed`/`chamfer-failed` and the edges of its faulty contours (`failedPaths`). Blend faces are named `blend:<edge name>` from `Generated`. Shell (`BRepOffsetAPI_MakeThickSolid`, by join with intersections) names each offset face `inner/<outer face>` from `Generated`; OCCT gives the edges it builds a fixed 1e-3 mm tolerance, which the evaluator limits to the result tolerance (`ShapeFix_ShapeTolerance`) and keeps only if the shape still checks. Draft (`BRepOffsetAPI_DraftAngle`) tilts faces about the plane through the neutral face's centroid normal to the pull direction, and refuses a neutral face off that plane (`draft-neutral-plane`). Pattern copies the tool by `BRepBuilderAPI_Transform` (`count` copies, linear or circular), names copy i's faces `copy:<pattern ID>:<i>/…` and Booleans all copies with the target at once; a cut copy whose common part with the target has no solid fails with `pattern-no-overlap`. Mirror reflects about a plane or a planar face, whose plane comes from the nodes of a mesh of the face (`mirror-face-not-planar` otherwise), prefixes `mirror:<mirror ID>` and optionally unites the reflection with its input. Features and sketches are evaluated in one dependency order (`dependencyOrder`). A sketch region (R1d) becomes one planar face: its outer loop counter-clockwise and its holes clockwise whatever their listed order, points of one ID one vertex at the stored coordinates' tolerance, arcs from `gp_Circ` between those vertices; its sides are named `side:e:<element ID>`. A sketch attached to a face resolves that reference on the face's feature (`reference-missing`/`reference-ambiguous` by the sketch's ID), refuses a face that is not planar (`sketch-face-not-planar`) and takes the frame `attachFrame` gives for the face's plane and outward normal; a placement a sketch is attached to never takes the literal-placement fast path. R1e lofts (`BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections`) and sweeps (`BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell`, Frenet or a fixed trihedron) take each region's outer wire in its sketch's frame; their faces are `start`, `end` and `side:e:<element ID>` (a loft's sides by its first section; a sweep side also `path:e:<path element ID>`). A result that is not one valid solid, has no volume, or a sweep whose path turns tighter than the profile reaches from its start, fails as `loft-failed`/`sweep-failed`. Each body's mesh carries `topology` (faces with name, centroid and area; edges with their faces, end vertices, centroid and length, in the kernel's map order), `faceBase`/`edgeBase` for its evaluation-scoped pick ids and edge polylines with `edgeIds`. Builds without the R1 bindings (kept for old documents) evaluate as before without names or edges. The opt-in `timing` callback reports each feature's and each body's time for the performance harness. Sheets (R2a-1): `extrudeSurface` (`BRepPrimAPI_MakePrism` of the chain's wire) and `revolveSurface` (`BRepPrimAPI_MakeRevol`) give a shell whose faces are `side:e:<element ID>`; a sheet feature must give one valid shell of connected faces and no solid (`result-kind`, `sheet-disconnected`, `invalid-sheet`), and a sheet body reports area, bounds and its free edges (count and length; seams and poles are not free) instead of a volume. STL (`writeStl`) refuses a sheet (`sheet-not-solid`); the mesh check lets a sheet be open but still consistently oriented; GLB writes it double-sided. STEP writes a sheet as `shell_based_surface_model`, and `importStep` reads one shell without a solid (open or closed) as a sheet and refuses several shells (`step-multiple-shells`). Splines (R2a-2): a spline element becomes a `Geom_BSplineCurve` in its sketch's plane from its stored control points and `splineKnots` (periodic when closed), bounded by the vertices of its end points; one that crosses itself is refused (`sketch-self-intersection`), and a sweep path's tightest turn includes a spline's smallest radius of curvature from samples. Body volume is integrated by Gauss–Kronrod over the knot spans (`metrics.ts`, exact on B-spline faces too); the area keeps OCCT's fixed-order integration, within 5 × 10⁻⁴ on B-spline faces. Surface lofts and sweeps (R2a-2b): `surfaceLoft` (`BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` without a solid, through each chain's wire) and `surfaceSweep` (`BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell` without `MakeSolid`) name their faces as the solid ones do, and every surface feature names its free edges' boundaries (`start` and `end` where the chain is swept from and to, a loft's first and last chain; `edge:start:e:<first element>` and `edge:end:e:<last element>` where an open chain's ends sweep), carried with the faces through placements and mirrors; the table gives a boundary to a free edge only, never to a seam or pole. R2b-1: the catalog port's `evaluatePart` evaluates a part version with the assembly's build Worker only (exact, or a reference display for an approved substitute; otherwise `part-build-mismatch`). R2b-2a: an evaluation request may name `connectors` (references on the active bodies' results); the result returns each one's frame from the exact surface or curve (`connector-frames.ts`, read through `Geom_Surface`/`Geom_Curve` since this build cannot construct the adaptors): a planar face's centroid and outward normal, a cylindrical or conical face's axis through its centroid's foot, a circular edge's centre and normal, x by the sketch attachment rule; anything else is `unsupported`, and an unresolved name `ambiguous`/`missing` with candidates. The literal-placement cache ignores the assembly fields, which never change the document's own shapes. R2b-2c: the pick table lists each vertex once with its exact point (`topology.vertices`, in the order edges index them), and a vertex connector's frame is that point with the part's own axes. R2b-4b: with `coarse`, each body also gets a coarse display mesh (`lod: 'coarse'`, 1% of the bounding-box diagonal and 1 rad, `meshLod`; triangles only, without a pick table or edges), made after everything that reads the standard triangulation and appended after the standard meshes; it is not in the geometry digest and does not change the evaluation. R2b-5a (`step-assembly.ts`): a `step-assembly` request writes an assembly's plan (`assemblyStepPlan` in `cad-core`) as STEP AP242 through XDE: each shaped product from its version's BRep (`exportBrep`) once, with its bodies' colours, each instance as a component with its placement; `write.step.schema` is set to 5 (AP242) for that writer only and restored after, so the single-body STEP stays AP214. XDE writes a label's name as both product id and name, so the file's products and instances are renamed from placeholders afterwards in one pass (`p21String`); `assemblyStepReport` checks the file and counts products and instances. R2b-5b (`step-assembly-import.ts`): `importStepAssembly` scans the file (`scanStep` with `assembly`, which also accepts AP242 and lists the PRODUCT names, ids and descriptions), reads a copy whose PRODUCT names are per-entity placeholders through XDE (`STEPCAFControl_Reader` with names), so each label resolves to its own PRODUCT's id, name and description, and walks the tree from each free shape, flattening every instance with its composed placement; a product's solids (or, without one, its connected shells) are checked as the single import checks them, written once each as a BRep and split as `name (n)` when there are several, products without a shape are skipped. It refuses more than 1,024 instances while reading (`step-instance-limit`), nesting past 8 (`step-depth-limit`), a scaled or mirrored placement (`step-instance-transform`) and a file without a placed shape (`step-assembly-empty`); colours are not read.
- Boundary surfaces and fills (R2a-2c): a boundary surface is a Coons patch (`GeomFill_BSplineCurves`) of its curves raised to degree 3, an arc given as cubic pieces of 1/32 turn or less (non-rational; the radius within 1e-9 of itself); a referenced edge must be a line or a circle's arc (`boundary-curve-unsupported` otherwise). A fill is `BRepFill_Filling` through its curves, `G1` to the face beside a referenced free edge; when the plate's normal stays more than 0.1° off it fails with `fill-g1-unreached` and every `G1` side (the build reports only the largest error). Curves that do not close a loop in their order fail with `boundary-open`. Each boundary edge is named by the curve it came from, so rotating or reversing the order keeps the names; a loft section of referenced edges names its faces `from:<feature ID>/side:(<edge name>)`.
- Trim, sew and thicken (R2a-3): a trim splits the sheet with `BRepAlgoAPI_Splitter` against a prism of the sketch's chain, a plane's face or the other sheet, and keeps the pieces with 90% or more of their area on the stored side (`trim-no-cut`, `trim-side-undetermined`, `trim-empty` otherwise); cut edges are `sketch:<id>/cut:e:<element>`, `cut:plane` or `from:<feature>/cut:(<face>)`. A sew is `BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing` at the tolerance plus 0.000001 mm; when the result is not one shell, or a solid still has free edges, it sews again at 1 mm and fails with `sew-gap` and the input edges that join only there, else `sew-disconnected` or `sew-not-closed`. Every face is named `sew:<input feature>/<name>`. A thicken is `MakeThickSolidBySimple` (`both`: offset by half first); the face on the normal's side keeps the name, the other is `inner/<name>`, and each side face is `side:(<free edge>)`; a creased sheet exceeds the result tolerance and fails with `thicken-failed`. An error evaluation may carry `edges` (feature and name).
- Continuity (R2a-4a, `continuity.ts`): an evaluation may also sample one edge, named by its index in a body's pick table (`continuity`, display only; `WorkerKernelPort.analyzeContinuity` and `CatalogKernelPort.analyzeContinuity` for exact builds). Each of the 65 points of the edge's curve is projected onto both faces' surfaces by Newton steps from the edge's triangulation nodes, and `Geom_Surface::D2` gives the oriented normals and the normal curvatures across the edge (this build binds no surface adaptor, so `LocalAnalysis` and `BRepLProp_SLProps` are not used). An edge without two faces answers `continuity-free-edge`, `continuity-seam`, `continuity-degenerate`, `continuity-nonmanifold` or `continuity-edge-missing`.
- Curvature (R2a-4b, `curvature.ts`): an evaluation may also give each mesh the mean and Gauss curvature at its nodes (`curvature`, from the faces' surfaces at the nodes' parameters; NaN at a pole; not part of the geometry digest) or sample one edge's curvature comb (`comb`: 129 points of its 3D curve with the curvature and the normal toward the centre). `analyzeCurvature` and `analyzeComb` run them on the export slot of exact builds. A pole answers `curvature-degenerate`, an edge without a 3D curve `curvature-no-curve`, one not in the table `curvature-edge-missing`.
- `createKernelHost` / `worker.ts`: Worker-side handler. One job at a time; cancel is checked at macrotask boundaries between features and acknowledged after handles are released. A `progress` message names each feature and each body before its mesh.
- `WorkerKernelPort`: `CadKernelPort` for `CadCoordinator`. One running job and the latest waiting job; unacknowledged cancel (500 ms), job timeout (30 s) or a crash terminates the Worker and the next job starts on a fresh instance. A timeout names the feature or body from the last `progress` as `featureId`.

`equivalence.test.ts` writes the digests of every fixture's evaluation, meshes and STEP bytes to `CAD_EQUIVALENCE_OUT` (CAD60: compare before and after a change to the kernel packages). `kernel.test.ts` runs against the real build only when `CAD_OCCT_DIST` points to the development kernel (`scripts/cad-kernel`); see verification.
